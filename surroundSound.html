UX/UI Design

Surround Sound

An all-in-one concert discovery and ticketing platform that helps music fans find events, purchase tickets, and connect with other fans through community-driven forums and personalized recommendations.

Problem

The Challenge of Concert Discovery

Concert lovers often miss events due to scattered information, multiple ticketing platforms, and no centralized hub for discovery and community. Fans want an all-in-one platform to find concerts, buy tickets, and connect with other fans — reducing frustration and making live music more social.

Goals
  • Simplify concert discovery with a personalized feed.
  • Centralize ticket purchasing within the app.
  • Build a fan-driven community for tips and planning.
My Contribution

My Role & Responsibilities

As one of the designers on a collaborative team, my responsibilities included:

  • User Research: Conducted interviews to understand how concert-goers discover events and what frustrates them.
  • Interaction Design: Designed early wireframes and user flows exploring navigation across events, artists, and venues.
  • High-Fidelity UI Design: Developed polished mockups in Figma with vibrant visual direction and clear hierarchy.
  • Prototype Development: Built interactive prototypes to test navigation patterns and validate core flows.
  • Usability Improvements: Iterated on layouts and interactions based on peer feedback and user insights.
Research

Competitive Analysis

I compared Spotify, Bandsintown, and Eventbrite to understand where current tools succeed and fall short. None integrates all aspects of the concert experience in one place — this gap guided the core idea behind Surround Sound.

Findings

Key Insights

Scattered Discovery Ticketing Frustration Community Gap
Music fans miss events relying on Instagram, TikTok, and friends. Switching between multiple platforms causes confusion and missed opportunities. Users want forums for outfits, parking tips, and meetups missing from competitors.
"If I didn't follow fan accounts, I would have missed a lot." "Ticketmaster is just really stressful… queues are long." "I wish there was a forum for outfits and parking tips."

I created an empathy map to combine what users think, feel, say, and do around concerts and ticketing. It revealed key emotions like fear of missing events and confusion during ticket decisions, helping us recognize the user mindset before designing.

Ideation

User Flows

We brainstormed ways to improve concert discovery, ticketing, and fan engagement, then narrowed ideas to three key features: Social Feed, Ticket Purchase, and Info Page.

Design

Design Process

Outcomes

Impact

Through usability testing, I identified key frustrations with navigation, missing feedback, and unexpected flows. Based on these insights, we refined the prototype to make the experience smoother and more intuitive.

Unexpected Actions

Post and Add Tags buttons redirected users to the cart. Remapped flows — Post returns to the feed, Add Tags suggests hashtags.

Feedback & Confirmation

Users weren't sure if posts or ticket orders went through. Added confirmation pop-ups for posting, adding to cart, and checkout.

Leaving & Backtracking

No clear way to exit pages like Create Post or Info Page. Introduced a Back/Leave button in the top bar for easy navigation.

Learnings

Reflection

This project taught me the importance of designing not just for usability, but for meaning. After sharing my work with UX designers from T-Mobile, I learned that color choices should go beyond aesthetics — red, for example, often signals warning or error. In future iterations I would refine the palette and focus on designs that are both user-friendly and developer-friendly. Surround Sound showed me how community-driven features can transform an experience.
